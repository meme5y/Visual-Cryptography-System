# Visual Cryptography: Technology Overview

## Introduction
Visual cryptography is a cryptographic technique that uses visual patterns, colors, or light intensity to encode and transmit information. Unlike traditional digital communication, it relies on the human visual system or optical sensors as the receiver.

## Technical Background

### History
- **1994**: First proposed by Moni Naor and Adi Shamir
- **Early 2000s**: Applied to image sharing and watermarking
- **2010s**: Emergence of Li-Fi (Light Fidelity)
- **Present**: Educational and IoT applications

### Principles
1. **Spatial encoding**: Information encoded in light patterns
2. **Temporal encoding**: Information encoded in timing of light changes
3. **Color encoding**: Information encoded in RGB color values

## Implementation Approaches

### 1. LED-Based Systems
- **Advantages**: Low cost, easy implementation
- **Limitations**: Limited bandwidth, ambient light interference
- **Applications**: Short-range data transfer, educational tools

### 2. Display-Based Systems
- **Advantages**: Higher bandwidth, existing infrastructure
- **Limitations**: Requires camera/sensor, synchronization challenges
- **Applications**: Screen-to-camera communication

### 3. Laser-Based Systems
- **Advantages**: Long range, high precision
- **Limitations**: Safety concerns, alignment requirements
- **Applications**: Secure point-to-point communication

## Our Implementation

### Key Features:
- **Multi-speed transmission**: Adaptable to different conditions
- **Visual feedback**: Real-time transmission visualization
- **Auditory feedback**: Multi-sensory learning experience

- **Bit rate**: 1-10 bps (depending on speed setting)
- **Range**: Up to 2 meters in ideal conditions
